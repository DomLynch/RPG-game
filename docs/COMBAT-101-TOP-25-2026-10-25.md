# Combat 101: the top 25 for the gladiator game with stolen body parts

Strategy, 2026-10-25. For Dom, the OpenTrident dev, the Combat lane and the Leads.
Sources: the Hellish Quart vs Half Sword comparison (`opentrident-steam/docs/parts-lab/combat-physics-comparison-hellish-quart-vs-half-sword.md`), the 35 game studies (`opentrident-steam/docs/STUDY-*-combat.md`), Frankendom's combat reference and spec (`frankendom-combat/docs/COMBAT_REFERENCE.md`, `frankendom-strategy/GAME_SPEC.md`), the Games-Analysis learnings, and the grafting and wounds briefs.

## The shape of the game in five lines

1. Arena fights, player against NPCs first with duels for fun, phone-first at 375, deterministic sim, animation-driven, physics dresses the aftermath. That is Frankendom's nearly finished Gladiator Pit game and engine, kept whole: 100 bosses with their own AI, named bots, clip and share, finishers, controls, loot screen. V2 adds the physics, the new graphics and the scalable bodies on top. The point of the game is the visuals: a Japanese schoolgirl with thin legs, an ogre's fat stomach and a minotaur or dog face. Attributes and skills come after and differentiate further.
2. Every opponent is a bag of parts: head, torso, arms, hands, legs, feet, each with its gear. Kill it, take a part, wear it. The part brings its reach, mass, armour and one move.
3. Content is a factory, not hand work: hundreds of MakeHuman bodies, 20 monster archetypes, generated armour and weapons, 2,400 Mixamo clips plus our finishers, one rig.
4. Physics is one dial, motor strength: full for alive, dipped for stagger, zero for dead. The sim decides every outcome; phones only show it.
5. The one thing nobody else has: the body you fight with is the body you built from kills, and it fights differently because of it.

## Order of play

1. Now, in parallel: M1 motor bake-off (OpenTrident dev); parts contract rows for HP budgets, reach and mass (Combat with the Make Human dev's measure rows); first 50 factory opponents.
2. After M1: M2 role table, M3 death ramp and stagger dip, one 375 clip to Dom at the game camera.
3. After Dom's clip: M4 rule rows (zones, armour, dismember levels, knockdown counter), M5 severing through the builder's neck cut, the three-mode arena test (baseline, hybrid, mismatched anatomy).
4. Then Hellish Quart clips, then multiplayer duels on the Frankendom presence stack.

What this buys: Elden Ring's responsiveness, Half Sword's brutality on deaths, and a mechanic neither has, a fighter whose reach, weight and moves are the sum of what it killed.

## Addendum, 2026-10-25 evening: after the three-game comparison (Hellish Quart, Half Sword, Gladio and Glory)

| # | Insight | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **One strength number runs the whole combat: tip speed times sharpness, three rungs, hit over 20, stick over 50, kill or cut over 100** (Gladio, readable code). Our numbers are measured on our own clips first, never copied. | Item 17 adopts the three-rung scale. The M4 tuning page is one scale, not five systems. Experiment 1 (tip-speed histogram on our Mixamo and Hellish Quart attack clips) is the gate before any threshold is written. |
| A2 | **The body-part mechanic falls out of the strength number for free.** Tip speed is arm length times angular speed; the clip gives the angular speed, the part gives the length and the mass. A long or heavy arm swings a faster, heavier tip on the same clip. | Items 9 and 10 stop being separate stat rows. Reach, strength and stagger resistance are derived from the measure rows of the equipped parts through the one formula. No stat touches timing, still. The stability budget (fairness) prices parts by the strength they add. |
| A3 | **A dead body is cut by any hit from a dismember-capable weapon** (Gladio). This is the loot mechanic, not a menu. | The take-one loot screen becomes the butcher's table: the corpse lies in the arena, the player taps the part and the fighter cuts it with the authored finisher cut, physics detaches it with its gear. Same sim rule, zero new systems, and the most memorable thirty seconds of the game. Web and Finishers own it after M5. |
| A4 | **Knockdown and kill are two different counters** and the games that mix them feel wrong. Gladio's cumulative-damage-since-last-fall counter (60 of 120, resets on each fall, heavy weapons force the fall) for knockdown; Hellish Quart's per-zone budgets for kill and limp. | Item 16 fixed: both counters, each with one job. Ogres and elephants never fall; they only die. Our XL archetypes get the same flag. |
| A5 | **Armour is a threshold multiplier on one scale and the schema forbids a block field** (Dom's ruling, made unbreakable). The design check: every dismember-capable weapon class must have a top blow above 2.5 times the cut base, or top armour is a block in practice. Blunt weapons hurt through armour and force the fall instead of cutting. | Item 11 carries the five-tier table from the comparison (cloth 1.0 to top plate 0.35 cut, threshold 1.0 to 2.5). A lint rejects any immune or block field. Never copy Gladio's "armour raises HP and damage", that is the Myth of Empires power-creep trap. |
| A6 | **Stiffness is per state, not fixed:** idle 0.5, attack 0.5 to 0.7, roll 0.4, fall 0.1, dead 0.01 (both Puppet Master games). | The motor-strength dial becomes a per-state row on the clip tags. Phase 2 active ragdoll is then a row change, not a project. |
| A7 | **Ragdolls want a faster step than the sim.** Gladio steps physics at 100 Hz with 6 solver iterations; a thrown blade at 30 m/s tunnels a 10 cm capsule at 60 Hz. | M1 variant: ragdoll substeps at 120 Hz with 6 iterations inside the 60 Hz sim, measured on the phone budget. Only thrown weapons and cuts need it; standing ragdolls stay at 60. |
| A8 | **Server-authoritative collisions only** (Gladio multiplayer collides on the server). | Confirms our rule for duels and for the Armagedom port: phones never decide a hit or a cut. |

**Three kinds of kill clip and the finisher trigger (Dom's definitions and idea, Strategy ruling with the OpenTrident dev, 2026-10-25 ~17:xx):**
- FINISHER = the scripted wow moment when someone dies (decapitation, dismemberment, execution, stomp, backstab kill); killer move and victim death play as one scene. SPECIAL = a power move or big hit that may end in a finisher or a normal death. DEATH = a regular death from an ordinary hit, victim only. Classified by what it LOOKS like. Data name: `kind: finisher | special | death`, everywhere.
- Trigger: killing blows only (a part budget emptied per items 7 and 8). Chance 25% base + 1 point per 1% overkill (strength past the kill rung of the A1 scale, as percent of the part budget), cap 60%. The roll is drawn from the sim seed (deterministic, replayable), never a random call. ALWAYS 100% on the match-winning kill and on any boss kill. Pity: after 3 kills without a finisher the next is guaranteed; no "never twice in a row".
- Which finisher: one DATA table, weapon class x hit zone x victim state, no code branching; armour row raises the dismember threshold only. Otherwise the death scene is picked by hit zone (Hellish Quart's 26 named death clips).
- Bleed-out (B4) and finishers meet here: a finisher on a bleeding opponent ends the timer early and is the better-looking win.
