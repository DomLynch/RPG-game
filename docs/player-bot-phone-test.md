# First-time phone combat check

Use five people who have not played Frankendom. Give each the same phone build and a short normal-speed fight without debug text. Do not explain a move before asking what they saw. Ask permission before recording a person or their voice; game-only capture is enough for this check.

For each person, ask them to move into range, defend one attack, then try to punish it. After the exchange, ask: “What was the opponent doing before the hit?”, “What did you try to do?”, “Why did damage happen?”, and “What would you do next?” Repeat once for an overhead, side cut, thrust and wall warning if the fight presents them. Do not force an opponent action or count a cue that never appeared.

Record the device and orientation, opponent, move seen, chosen thumb action, whether the input registered, defence result, next attack result, and their own explanation. Record “named before contact” only if the person actually said it before impact; label an answer given afterward “retrospective.” Note thumb slips or simultaneous-control trouble separately from cue confusion and tactical choice. Mark “not seen” rather than a failure when no cue occurred.

Review the five notes together. Fix the cue most often misunderstood before changing balance. Browser-bot wins measure repeatable mechanics; they do not validate phone comfort, sound or first-time understanding.
