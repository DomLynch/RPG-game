# Local combat test player

Build once (`npm run build`), then run `node scripts/player-bot.mjs`. The default batch uses the tactical strategy against Pitborn on Easy at three deterministic seeds derived from 731. Options: `--strategy=tactical|charged|counter`, `--observation=debug|limited`, `--opponent=pitborn`, `--opponents=all` (all playable opponents), `--seed=731`, `--fights=3` (maximum 12), `--reaction-ms=180` (100–700), `--step-ms=32`, `--headed`, `--no-video`, `--no-clips`, `--show-debug-video`, `--out=artifacts/combat/player-bot`. `--smoke` runs one fight. The old charged and counter strategies remain available for diagnostic comparison; the standalone launcher pins the tactical strategy.

The controller uses the normal browser keyboard path. Charged mode approaches, charges Heavy, answers readable tells and recovers stamina. Counter mode approaches under directional guard, waits for a confirmed block, presses Heavy for the guard counter, and may stab after an opponent miss if within reach. Tactical mode leads with range-aware slash or thrust, reserves stamina to guard a reacted tell, and punishes observed misses or successful defence. It allows Heavy only after four actual non-heavy attack starts and an earned guard/counter opening. **Limited observation is the standard run** (`--observation=limited`, the default): the bot sees what a player sees. Combat events pass through a perception filter (`scripts/lib/player-bot-observation.mjs` `perceivable`): a swing seen starting (its side and whether it is a heavy, light, thrust or kick windup, never whether it is charged), contact sounds (block vs perfect block, parry, hit, guard break), whiffs, rolls and backsteps, and the charge sound as `ChargeCue` with no actor, because the one cue plays for either fighter's charge. Damage, stamina costs, charge flags, the chamber, feint/guard/parry presses and the opponent's exhaustion are dropped. The opponent counts as attacking from a seen swing start until a seen end of it (no hidden DOM flag is read; `#combat-status` `data-threat` has had no visual since #676). A charge may only be inferred from the charge sound while a seen heavy winds up and the bot is not holding its own, or from the hold time (a heavy still winding up past its plain windup + 8 ticks); the roll's reason names which (`(sound)` / `(hold time)`). Stamina comes from the meter. Every opponent-side signal is delayed by the reaction time, and distance/position are rounded to half-metre steps. Own health, stamina and action phase stay current. The tell is never read from the event-line text. `--observation=debug` is the secondary run: exact current gap, position, stamina and phase from the local overlay plus every combat event. Direction changes wait the configured reaction delay in every mode. It has no future state, damage override or direct simulation control. A local-only `debug=1&botSeed=N` URL selects the starting seed; public hosts ignore it.

Each fight writes `opponent-seed.json` (inputs, decisions and combat events with simulation ticks), optionally `opponent-seed.webm` (full fight plus five seconds of its conclusion, with a visible seed/tick label) and the compact batch `summary.json`. The measured fight ends at `endTick`; later video events are kept separately as `aftermathEvents` and cannot become fight highlights. Decision entries record intention, requested keys/press, phase and spacing, then whether an attack started and what happened next. A press with no matching start event is evidence of an unaccepted input, not proof of its cause. With video and `ffmpeg` available, up to four short MP4s per fight are cut around a missed attack, failed guard, wall punishment or long idle stretch. The debug panel is hidden in ordinary video but remains readable by the bot; `--show-debug-video` retains it in the full video while masking it in clips. The JSON lists each clip and any export error, plus sampled canvas geometry and a review-suitability flag if its bounds change. Clip speed is estimated from sampled video time versus simulation ticks. Playwright video is silent, so these clips cannot judge sound; use a real-time recording with audio for that review. `--no-video` skips videos and clips; `--no-clips` keeps only full videos. Match full-video frames to events using the on-video tick label and clip moments using the JSON tick. All runs, including losses and timeouts, are retained.

Receipts include actual attack starts, hits and damage by move, per-fight Heavy percentage, thrust attempts/hits/damage, missed player swings, estimated seconds near the wall (one-second position samples), seconds in contact-free stretches of at least five seconds, the longest contact gap, and the number of blocks/parries followed by a player hit before the next opponent hit within two seconds. Each `defenceExchanges` row connects an incoming attack to any new guard/roll input after the tell, resolved contact, actual damage, next chronological player hit and sampled gap/radius before and after. `beforeNextEnemyAttack` tells whether that hit belongs to the same exchange; otherwise do not credit it to the earlier defence. A null choice means no new defensive input was logged after that tell; the result can still show a block from an existing guard. An interrupted or feinted swing is labelled as such; a dodge shows demonstrated avoidance, not estimated damage saved. Spacing samples are about one second apart and are not exact contact positions. `damageSources` separates player and opponent direct hits, guard breaks and blocked chip from arena lashes. `damageDealt` is player-attributed damage only; `damageTaken` includes opponent damage and arena damage to the player. These are raw event amounts, which can exceed net health lost on an overkill. These are diagnostic measures, not a gameplay score. Held keys are released in cleanup on wins, losses, timeouts and errors; a loss also verifies that Rematch starts with keys released. A new fight uses a fresh browser context. The script exits nonzero unless the batch wins at least two-thirds (2/3) against each selected Easy opponent without runner errors; tactical mode also requires Heavy to be at most 20% of actual attack starts in every fight.

These are mechanical test results. Debug observations are more precise than a human's, and the browser viewport emulates a phone. Footage and physical phone playtests remain the evidence for readability and control feel.

**What each defence earned** (`defences` per fight, `report.<opponent>.defences` in `summary.json`): for every block, perfect block, parry, roll and backstep, the opponent attack it answered (if any), the damage avoided (the move's base damage, ×1.5 if charged, minus chip), whether a counter window opened (always for a block or parry; for an evade only when the swing went to air) and whether the bot started an attack inside it (guard counter 20 ticks, parry stun 90, evade 45, before the next opponent swing) and landed it, plus the change in gap (+ = space gained) and in distance from the centre (+ = toward the wall) over the next half-second. `chargedHeavies` lists how each of the opponent's charged heavies was answered (`correct` = rolled or parried, `guarded into` = blocked or guard broken, `other` = hit clean or missed on spacing) and which cue the bot's decision named (`sound`, `hold time`, `event` in debug, or `nothing`). These come from the exact event log after the fight and are never bot input.
